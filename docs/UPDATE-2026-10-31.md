## 3. Recipes cooked (newest first)

### 3.1 Deep-frying session – 30 Oct *(dish not recorded)*
First time deep-frying. Facts discussed: keep used oil (strain through a fine sieve or coffee filter, store closed, dark and cool, 3–5 more uses; discard when rancid, very dark or foaming), never pour oil down the sink (it solidifies in pipes, causes clogs and sewer fatbergs), dispose of it closed in Restmüll or at a recycling center. Fry sweet things last.

*(Claude Code chat, 29/30 Oct)*: the dish was **Hong Kong style sweet and sour chicken** for 2: boneless thighs in an egg, soy and starch batter, double-fried in the small pot (2.5 cm oil, about 170 °C), ketchup and pineapple-juice sauce with onion and paprika in the cast-iron pan, rice. His changes and stars are not recorded yet; the recipe is meant to become `recipes/` entry #2 with his amounts.

### 3.2 Egg drop soup – 29 Oct, 1 big bowl
Base: water, salt, MSG, starch, chicken powder. Suggested additions: ¼ TL white pepper (signature taste), a few drops of sesame oil off the heat, 1 TL light soy sauce, spring onions; optional ginger slice or a splash of Shaoxing.
Method notes: starch must be mixed into cold water first, then into simmering soup. Pour beaten egg in a thin stream while the soup moves in a circle, wait about 10 seconds, stir gently once or twice, turn off the heat.
Result: tasty, but opaque with very fine egg (stirred too soon/too fast or a lot of egg for the broth). For bigger ribbons: pour slowly from higher up, wait 10 seconds, pull the spoon through slowly. Starch makes it thicken as it cools; loosen with hot water.
Nutrition: basically egg + salty broth. To make it a meal: silken tofu, frozen peas or corn, spinach, tomato (tomato egg drop soup), shredded chicken, or eat it the Chinese way as a side soup with rice and dishes.

### 3.3 Deutscher Linseneintopf – 26 Oct, about 12 portions (frozen in containers)
His first German lentil stew (his grandmother never made it). Cost: about EUR 16 for him (a friend paid part), roughly EUR 1.30 per portion.

What was actually used:
- 1 kg **Berglinsen** (he first bought red lentils and exchanged them)
- 6 large carrots (all from the Suppengemüse pack; enough, more would make it sweet)
- about 350 g Knollensellerie (from a 732 g knob, rest kept in the fridge) plus the celery from the Suppengemüse
- 2 leeks plus the leek from the Suppengemüse
- 3 onions
- about 600 g potatoes (from a 2.5 kg bag)
- 2 × 125 g Katenschinkenwürfel
- Thüringer Knackwurst with caraway (bought by the friend; works well, but caraway)
- 2 EL tomato paste
- gekörnte Gemüsebrühe: 4 heaped EL to start (1 heaped EL ≈ 2 level EL)
- 3 bay leaves, 2 TL Majoran
- water (planned 3 l, less fit in the pot)
- balsamico at the end, pepper, salt last
- parsley from the Suppengemüse, mustard on the plate (cheap mittelscharf; Dijon is sharper, use less)
- No garlic: classic German lentil stew gets its flavor from smoke (bacon, sausage), Majoran, bay leaf and vinegar.
- No spinach in the end (not on the receipt).

Mise en place he used:
- Bowl 1: onions, fine dice
- Bowl 2: carrots + celeriac, 1 cm dice (go in together). He put the celeriac with the potatoes by accident; that is fine, it just cooks in the broth instead of being sautéed.
- Bowl 3: leek, halved lengthwise, washed, half rings
- Bowl 4: potatoes, 1 cm dice, in water
- Board: sausage in 1 cm slices
- Small dish: chopped parsley for the end
- No chopping: bacon cubes (pre-cut), lentils (just rinse)

Steps (Berglinsen version):
1. Brown the sausage slices in 1 EL oil, 2–3 min, take out.
2. Bacon in the sausage fat, 4–5 min.
3. Onions, 3 min.
4. Carrots (and celeriac), 5 min.
5. Leek, 2 min.
6. Tomato paste, toast 1 min.
7. Water, stock powder, bay leaves, Majoran, rinsed lentils. Boil, then simmer about 15 min, uncovered while the pot is very full (wooden spoon across the pot helps against boiling over). Skim the grey lentil foam if there is a lot; leave the fat.
8. Potatoes (and celeriac), simmer about 20 min more, stir along the bottom.
9. Sausage back for the last 5–10 min.
10. Remove bay leaves. Balsamico (about 3 EL), pepper (about ½ TL), then salt. Pepper and vinegar go in only at the end.
11. Parsley on top or on the plate.

What went wrong: not enough water fit in the pot, so the lentils stayed hard. He ate a few portions' worth, then added more water and simmered longer. The tomato paste's acid also slows lentils a little, which is why vinegar goes in last. Sausage went back in early; Knackwurst is firm and survived the extra simmering.
Storage: cooled quickly, split into containers in the fridge the same night; freeze what is not eaten within 3–4 days (about 3 months). Add water when reheating (lentils keep absorbing). Mustard on the plate, not in the pot.

Next time: 750 g lentils for his pot (or a bigger pot), keep 6 carrots, Mettenden or Wiener if the caraway-hating friend eats along.

Regional variants (for notes): Swabian Linsen mit Spätzle und Saitenwürstle (thicker, Mehlschwitze), Rhineland sweet-sour (vinegar plus a pinch of sugar), with Kasseler or Rippchen.

Micronutrients per portion (rough estimate, for the Nährwichtel feature): folate about 400 µg (about the full daily need), iron 6–7 mg (about 60% for men), vitamin A from carrots (about half), vitamin B1 (more than half), potassium, magnesium and zinc (each a quarter to a third), vitamin K from leek and parsley, about 10 g fiber. Downside: 2–3 g salt per portion from bacon, sausage and stock powder. Tip: vitamin C with the meal helps iron absorption, tea or coffee with the meal hurts it.

### 3.4 Saté chicken with peanut sauce and rice – sauce and chicken made on 20 Oct as far as this chat shows (originally planned for his mother's visit on Thursday 22 Oct)
Origin: Indonesia (probably Java), based on meat skewers brought by Indian and Arab traders; peanuts only reached Southeast Asia from the Americas in the 1500s.

Peanut sauce, double batch (about 8 portions), freezes well (3 months, may separate, whisk with a splash of water when reheating; freeze in ice cube trays):
- 1 EL oil, 2 EL red Thai curry paste, ½ TL garlic powder (fresh garlic was moldy; ¼ TL powder ≈ 1 clove)
- 200 g peanut butter, almost 400 ml coconut milk (the rest of the can after the marinade)
- 2 EL light soy sauce, 2 EL sugar, 2 EL vinegar (apple vinegar best; with Sushi-Su use only 1 EL sugar; white wine vinegar 1 EL; no balsamico)
- Steps: fry the curry paste 1 min, add coconut milk and garlic powder, stir in peanut butter on low heat, add soy/sugar/vinegar, simmer 5 min, thin with water, taste (too sweet: vinegar; bland: soy; too hot: sugar or peanut butter). Garlic powder burns in hot oil, add it with the liquid.

Marinade for 800 g chicken breast (Hähnchenfilet), 1 cm strips against the grain, 15–30 min:
- 3 EL light soy sauce, 1½ EL oil, 1½ TL sugar, 1½ TL turmeric, 1½ TL ground coriander, ½ TL garlic powder, 3 EL coconut milk (optional; without it use ½ EL more oil)
- Cooking: very hot pan, 1 EL oil, half the meat at a time in one layer, 2–3 min without moving, turn, 2 more min. Sugar darkening on the pan is normal.
- Timing trick when the meat arrives fresh: marinade ready in the fridge, cut and marinate while the rice cooks.
- Side: cucumber salad with 2 EL vinegar, 1 TL sugar, pinch of salt.

### 3.5 Sinangag-style garlic fried rice – 19 Oct, 1 portion
Day-old rice (cooled fast, kept in the fridge), fried in the saved chicken fat from the Adobo, with 2 garlic cloves, spring onion (white first, green at the end), 1 egg, 1 EL light soy sauce, sesame oil and Lao Gan Ma optional. Served with the last Adobo thigh. With adobo and egg this is the Filipino breakfast *adosilog* (adobo + sinangag + itlog = egg).
Order: fat, garlic and spring onion white 1 min (do not brown), rice 3–4 min, push rice aside and scramble the egg 20–30 s, mix, light soy sauce poured at the pan edge, finish with green onion, sesame oil, Lao Gan Ma. Light soy sauce for taste, dark soy only for color (1 TL max).
Rice safety: cooked rice is fine for 1–2 days if cooled within about 1 hour and kept closed in the fridge below 5 °C; reheat once until steaming hot. Bacillus cereus toxins survive frying.

### 3.6 Chicken Adobo – 18 Oct *(amounts not recorded; recipe already in his old recipe book)*
Bone-in chicken legs (bought reduced by 30%), marinated 30 min, seared, braised in soy sauce and vinegar on the stovetop (plate as a makeshift lid), ground pepper instead of peppercorns, served with white rice and no vegetables. Fat skimmed and saved (used next day for fried rice).
Verdict: tasty sauce, but he did not enjoy the bones and gristle. Not a dish he wants to repeat soon. If ever again: boneless thighs with skin, braise about 20 min instead of 35–40.
Lessons: aluminum foil and vinegar react (the foil corrodes, do not cover acidic dishes with foil), skim foam and fat during braising, adobo bones make a salty-sour broth if ever boiled (collect bones in the freezer for a real stock).
Background: adobo is pre-colonial Filipino vinegar-and-salt preservation; the Spanish named it; soy sauce came via Chinese traders. Not related to *pagpag* (scavenged fast-food chicken, a modern poverty practice in Manila).

Storage facts confirmed in this period:
- Toasted sesame oil: cupboard is fine (6–12 months opened, dark, not above the stove). Fridge makes it cloudy and thick (harmless).
- Mirin: fridge. Real hon-mirin up to 1 year; mirin-style seasoning (aji-mirin, what he has) 3–6 months. Sugar crystals are harmless.
- Cooking sake 料理酒 *ryōrishu* (Morita): "Reiswein zum Kochen" on the German label is the same thing as "cooking sake". 酒 *sake* just means alcohol in Japanese; drinking sake is 日本酒 *nihonshu*. Contains salt. Refrigerate after opening (開栓後要冷蔵, below 10 °C).
- Tomato paste: tube 4–6 weeks in the fridge; jar or can 5–7 days (about 2 weeks under a layer of oil); best: freeze in EL dollops.
- Ground spices lose aroma after 1–2 years.
- Peanut sauce freezes about 3 months.
- Lentil stew: fridge 3–4 days, freezer about 3 months.

## 7. Cooking facts learned (add to the knowledge/tips section)

- Garlic germ (green sprout) is not harmful; slightly bitter when old. Burning garlic is the real problem.
- Garlic breath: milk with the meal, apple or parsley afterwards help a little.
- Red lentils fall apart (curry, thick soups, 10–15 min); Tellerlinsen and Berglinsen keep their shape (stews, 25–35 min). Berglinsen are small, green-brown, nutty, 1:1 for Tellerlinsen. Lentil protein: about 23 g per 100 g dry.
- Acid (tomato paste, vinegar) slows lentils from softening; add vinegar only at the end.
- Kümmel = caraway; Kreuzkümmel = cumin (different spices).
- Caraway, anise, fennel, star anise, Estragon and licorice share a flavor family.
- Thüringer Knackwurst is a cured, smoked Rohwurst and can be eaten cold.
- Airfryer vegetables get soggy mainly from overcrowding, wet vegetables, too low a temperature (use 190–200 °C), salting too early, watery vegetables, not shaking; 1 EL oil tossed in a bowl helps browning.
- Deep-frying: see 3.1. Never pour oil into the sink.
- Egg drop soup is a side soup in China, shared from one bowl with rice and dishes.
- Satay origin and adobo origin: see 3.4 and 3.6.
- Heaped EL ≈ 2 level EL.
